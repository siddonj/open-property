# Fix the Rent Ledger Before Adding Accounting

A general ledger for open-property is buildable on D1, but the compliance work sits in three places: tenant receivables, security deposits, and ledger controls. These are not in the chart of accounts or the reports, which are mostly configuration and SQL. **Today's schema breaks the preconditions for all three.** Money is stored as `REAL`. Rent payments are hard-deleted and recomputed into a mutable `amount_paid`. Each payment is tied to exactly one charge. Deleting a property cascades through leases, charges and payments. The deposit is a number on the lease. There are no users. The `@clawnify/db` adapter exposes only single-statement `query/get/run`, so a multi-line posting cannot currently be written atomically. The right v1 is an integer-cents, append-only, double-entry ledger with these parts:

- a HUD-shaped chart of accounts with property and unit as dimensions
- an open-item tenant receivables subledger whose payment-application and late-fee rules are driven by the property's state
- a security-deposit liability subledger with a per-jurisdiction rules table and return-deadline tracking
- line-coded vendor bills that capture capex and 1099 facts
- period locks
- a standard report set: trial balance, P&L to NOI, balance sheet, rent roll, aged receivables and deposit activity, with cash basis derived from payment applications

Trust accounting, deposit interest, GPR journal posting, voucher payer-splitting and lender exports come later. HUD/LIHTC certification, TRACS, REAC filing, payment processing and tax-return preparation stay out. One decision has to come first. The roadmap in `intent.md` lists "a full general ledger" as out of scope, and the draft Phase 3 spec (`docs/sdlc/changes/phase-3-financial/spec.md`) would add a `REAL`-valued, cascade-deleted `expenses` table. If a GL is coming, change that migration now. Rewriting it after it ships costs a data migration.

## Five schema defects block a trustworthy ledger today

Every mature ledger the research examined shares one shape. An account, a journal-entry header and journal lines are written append-only, and corrections are offsetting entries, never updates. Square's Books service has "no update statements… only inserts" and keeps mistakes in the audit trail by offsetting them ([Square](https://developer.squareup.com/blog/books-an-immutable-double-entry-accounting-database-service/)). Modern Treasury warns that "mutating balances directly creates a system that is prone to errors" ([Modern Treasury](https://www.moderntreasury.com/journal/accounting-for-developers-part-i)). TigerBeetle stores integer amounts at a fixed scale and notes that **scales "are hard to change later"** ([TigerBeetle](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/coding/data-modeling/)). Measured against that, the current code has five structural gaps.

The first is money type. Every amount in `src/server/schema.sql` (`rent_charges.amount`, `payments.amount`, `leases.deposit`, `units.market_rent`) is `REAL`. A ledger needs `INTEGER` cents and a string-to-cents parser, because `0.29*100` is not 29 in IEEE-754. The second is mutability. `POST /api/payments` and `DELETE /api/payments/:id` in `src/server/index.ts` insert or hard-delete a payment and then rewrite `rent_charges.amount_paid` and `status` in separate statements. History disappears, and a failure between statements leaves the charge inconsistent. Waivers are a status flip, not an entry. The third is the payment model. `payments.charge_id` is a single foreign key, so the system cannot represent one receipt covering rent plus a late fee, an unapplied prepayment, or an NSF reversal that reopens several charges. The fourth is cascade deletes. `units`, `leases`, `rent_charges` and `payments` all `ON DELETE CASCADE` from their parent, so deleting a property erases its financial history. That contradicts record-retention minimums of **3–7 years** under IRS rules ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/how-long-should-i-keep-records)). The fifth is identity. The app is single-user with no auth (Phase 5 in `intent.md`), so `created_by`, approvals and segregation of duties have nothing to attach to.

The platform constraint matters as much as the schema. D1's only documented transaction mechanism is `batch()`: "Batched statements are SQL transactions," and a failure "aborts or rolls back the entire sequence" ([Cloudflare D1](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/worker-api/d1-database/)). The repository's adapter (`src/server/db.ts` re-exporting `@clawnify/db`) exposes neither `batch` nor `env.DB`. Its preview binding is a one-statement-per-RPC interface. That leaves two options for atomic postings. Option one is to extend the adapter to pass through D1 `batch()`, which works in production but not in the Facet preview. Option two is a "seal" pattern: insert the header unsealed, insert the lines, then flip `sealed=1` under a trigger that `RAISE(ABORT)`s if lines do not sum to zero, and have every report read only sealed entries. With the seal pattern, a failed posting leaves an invisible orphan rather than a half-posted entry. **Trigger and `RAISE` behavior on D1 is implied by its PRAGMA support but not documented**, so verify it locally before relying on it ([D1 SQL](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/sql-api/sql-statements/)).

The recommended core is small:

| Table | Purpose | Key constraints |
|---|---|---|
| `gl_accounts` | Natural accounts only | type, normal side, `is_control`, `is_operating`, `schedule_e_line`, `hud_code` |
| `gl_periods` | Monthly periods | `open` → `closed` → `locked`; reopen is audited |
| `journal_entries` | Header | `entry_date` (property-local `YYYY-MM-DD`), `created_at` (UTC), `source_type/source_id`, `reverses_id UNIQUE`, `idempotency_key UNIQUE`, `sealed` |
| `journal_lines` | Signed `amount_cents` (+debit/−credit) | `CHECK (amount_cents <> 0)`; dimensions `property_id`, `unit_id`, `lease_id`, `tenant_id`, `vendor_id`; no update/delete triggers |

Idempotency keys should come from the business event, such as `rent:{lease_id}:{YYYY-MM}`, so a re-run recurring job cannot double-bill. This mirrors TigerBeetle's use of client-supplied ids to make retries safe ([TigerBeetle](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/coding/data-modeling/)) and the existing `UNIQUE(lease_id, period)` on `rent_charges`. The accounting date must be the property's local date, because the current `paid_at` default of `datetime('now')` is UTC and will push late-evening month-end receipts into the next period. The project has no test suite, so the most valuable single check is an invariant endpoint. It should assert that every entry sums to zero and that AR, AP and deposit control accounts equal their subledgers per property.

## A HUD-shaped chart with dimensions, not property-coded accounts

There is no NAA, NMHC or IREM numbered multifamily chart of accounts. **The only published, defined, account-by-account standard is HUD's** (Handbook 4370.2, carried forward by state housing finance agencies). Missouri's MHDC version numbers assets 1xxx, liabilities 2xxx, equity 3xxx and revenue 5xxx, with vacancy contra-revenue at 52xx, operating expense at 62xx–68xx and owner-entity items at 71xx ([MHDC](https://mhdc.com/media/3illlhjy/mhdc-chart-of-accounts.pdf)). HUD itself says prescribed accounts exist so it can ingest statements "without any interpretation of the accounts" ([HUD 4370.2 Ch. 2](https://www.hud.gov/sites/documents/43702c2hsgh.pdf)). Seeding a HUD-shaped 4-digit chart costs nothing extra and makes affordable-housing and lender mapping a lookup rather than a rebuild. Conventional vendors use 4xxx revenue instead (Buildium, Rentec), so a `hud_code` mapping column lets one chart produce both formats ([Rentec Direct](https://help.rentecdirect.com/article/843-chart-of-accounts)).

Property belongs on the journal line, not in the account number. Buildium's blog suggests encoding the property in the last digit of child accounts ([Buildium](https://www.buildium.com/blog/chart-of-accounts-property-management)). Real-estate ERP consultants call that pattern the source of "thousands of near-duplicate accounts," because the same expense lands in different codes at different properties ([REdirect Consulting](https://www.redirectconsulting.com/blog/gl-coding-in-real-estate-how-the-general-ledger-and-chart-of-accounts-work-in-yardi-and-mri); [JMCO](https://www.jmco.com/articles/real-estate/why-your-yardi-chart-of-accounts-is-costing-you-time-every-month-and-how-to-fix-it/)). The existing `properties`, `units`, `leases`, `tenants`, `vendors` and `work_orders` tables map directly onto line dimensions. A per-property P&L is then a `GROUP BY`.

The revenue section needs a deliberate shape because lenders read it top-down. Fannie Mae's underwriting waterfall runs **GPR − vacancy − concessions − bad debt = net rental income, + other income = EGI, − operating expenses = NOI, − replacement reserve = NCF** ([Fannie Mae Guide §606](https://mfguide.fanniemae.com/fnmf-pdf/download/3996)). Practice splits on two points the chart must accommodate. HUD books bad debt as administrative expense (6370) and non-revenue units as departmental expenses (6312, 6331, 6521), keeping GPR at 100% ([MHDC](https://mhdc.com/media/3illlhjy/mhdc-chart-of-accounts.pdf)). Fannie deducts bad debt from rental income, and Freddie Mac's CREFC coding sends employee and model units to payroll and G&A rather than netting them against GPR ([Freddie Mac PRS CREFC Desk Reference](https://mf.freddiemac.com/docs/prs_crefc_desk_reference_guide.pdf)). Give each account `is_operating` and report-line attributes instead of relying on number ranges. Then mortgage interest, depreciation, capex, reserve deposits and owner-entity fees fall below NOI regardless of account number, and the HUD-versus-lender presentation becomes a mapping choice rather than a re-coding.

The basis question has a clean answer: **accrual is the source of truth, and cash basis is a derived view.** Small landlords overwhelmingly file cash-basis taxes, and IRS rules override book timing. Advance rent and lease-cancellation payments are income when received under either method, and refundable deposits are not income until kept ([IRS Pub 527](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527)). Posting every charge to AR and deriving cash-basis income from payment-application records (dated by receipt and grouped by the charge's revenue account) produces both views without double-posting. Yardi-style closes tie out against both an accrual and a cash trial balance ([REdirect Consulting](https://www.redirectconsulting.com/blog/yardi-year-end-close-checklist-2026-step-by-step-month-year-end-process)). The application table is therefore an accounting object, not a UI convenience.

## Receivables carry the densest state law and the trickiest GAAP

The tenant ledger needs three tables to work: charges, receipts, and many-to-many applications between them. Charges post Dr AR / Cr revenue, and receipts post Dr cash or undeposited funds / Cr AR. Applications drive aging and cash-basis income but do not hit the accrual GL. AR, like every control account, should accept postings only from subledger rules. Manual journal entries to control accounts are the classic cause of subledger drift ([ERPNext](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/general-ledger); [Stripe](https://stripe.dev/blog/ledger-stripe-system-for-tracking-and-validating-money-movement)).

Payment application order is regulated, so it cannot be a hard-coded default. **Washington requires that a landlord "must first apply any payment made by a tenant toward rent"** before late fees, damages or legal costs ([RCW 59.18.283](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=59.18.283)). Wisconsin does the same, according to legal aid ([wislawhelp](https://www.wislawhelp.org/print/pdf/node/424)). Oregon bars deducting a prior late charge from a current rent payment ([ORS 90.260](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_90.260)). Buildium's default, by contrast, is largest-balance-first with an operator-ranked override ([Buildium](https://buildium.com/blog/introducing-custom-payment-allocation)). The allocator therefore needs, in order: a state-mandated rent-first rule, operator priority, oldest due date, then largest balance. Charge codes need an `is_rent` flag to support it.

Late fees vary more. The existing global `late_fee_amount` and `late_fee_grace_days` settings cannot express them:

| State | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Texas | Fee only if rent is unpaid **two full days** after due; must be in a written lease; safe harbor ≤12% of rent (≤4 units) or ≤10% (>4 units); violation = $100 + 3× fee + attorney's fees | [Tex. Prop. Code §92.019](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._prop._code_section_92.019) |
| New York | Lesser of **$50 or 5%**, only after 5 days; NSF fee limited to actual bank cost | [N.Y. RPL §238-a](https://newyork.public.law/laws/n.y._real_property_law_section_238-a) |
| Oregon | Not before the 4th day; flat, daily (≤6% of flat), or 5% per 5-day period | [ORS 90.260](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_90.260) |

All seeded properties are in Austin, Texas. The 3-unit "308 Mission Apartments" falls in the 12% tier, so a $50 fee on $1,450 rent is compliant. A state rules table keyed on `properties.state`, validated when late-fee settings are saved, keeps it that way when operators add other states.

GAAP adds one trap that most homegrown systems get wrong. Under ASC 842 lessor accounting, when collection of a lease's payments stops being probable, the change "shall be recognized as a current-period adjustment to lease income." The write-off of rent receivables runs **through revenue, not bad-debt expense**, and operating-lease receivables are excluded from CECL ([Deloitte FRA 19-1](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/publications/archive/deloitte-publications/financial-reporting-alerts/2019/19-1-assessing-collectibility-operating-lease)). A general portfolio reserve is an optional, disclosed policy that may be shown in either revenue or bad-debt expense. The same Deloitte guidance notes that receivables from non-lease components may need the separate CECL model ([Deloitte FRA 19-1](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/publications/archive/deloitte-publications/financial-reporting-alerts/2019/19-1-assessing-collectibility-operating-lease)). For v1, the defensible implementation has three parts: an event-based write-off (move-out with balance, eviction, skip) that posts to a contra-revenue "bad debt – rent" account, a presentation toggle for HUD-style expense reporting, and a recovery path that re-opens AR before applying cash. The tenant's legal balance should survive in the subledger after the GL write-off so collections and move-out statements still show it. Straight-lining free-rent concessions is also GAAP (total net payments divided by the term) ([FinQuery](https://finquery.com/blog/rent-abatement-and-rent-free-period-accounting/)). Most small operators book concessions as contra-revenue when granted, though the notes found no source on whether that is commonly immaterial. Make it a per-owner policy flag that defaults to as-incurred, and do not build straight-line schedules in v1.

NSF and returned payments must reverse the original receipt and its applications, reopening the charges. They must never delete the receipt, which is exactly what `DELETE /api/payments/:id` does today. Unapplied cash is prepaid rent, a liability until the next charge consumes it. Gross potential rent, loss-to-lease and vacancy loss can be either posted to the GL (Yardi Breeze Premier and Infor PowerSite do this) or computed for reports only (Propertyware) ([Yardi Breeze](https://www.yardibreeze.com/refresh-virtual/classes/page/11); [Infor PowerSite](https://amsiaws.saas.infor.com/AMSIWEBG001/Areas/eSite/help/loss_to_lease_calculation_overview.htm); [Propertyware](https://support.propertyware.com/s/article/What-are-the-columns-option-for-the-Gross-Potential-Rent-Report)). Net income is identical either way, so ship report-only GPR first. One gap blocks even that: `units.market_rent` is a single current value. Historical GPR and loss-to-lease need an effective-dated market-rent history, so start recording it now.

## Security deposits are a liability with a statutory clock

Deposits are where a ledger bug becomes a statutory penalty. A refundable deposit is a liability while held and income only when kept. A "deposit" designated as last month's rent is advance rent and taxable on receipt ([IRS Pub 527](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527)). There is no national rule, and recent changes mean the rules table must be effective-dated and evaluated against the lease or collection date, not today's date:

| Jurisdiction | Cap | Holding | Return deadline | Penalty / note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| California | **1 month** for deposits collected on/after 2024-07-01 (small-landlord exception) | Commingling allowed; tenant has priority over creditors | 21 days | Cap includes pet/key deposits and advance rent ([AB 12](https://legiscan.com/CA/text/AB12/2023); [SF Rent Board](https://www.sf.gov/news/security-deposit-laws-are-changing-july-1-2024)) |
| Maryland | **1 month** incl. pet deposit, leases on/after 2024-10-01 | Interest-bearing insured account | 45 days | ([MD DHCD HB693 FAQ](https://dhcd.maryland.gov/TurningTheKey/Documents/HB693-FAQ.pdf)) |
| New York | 1 month | In trust; 6+ unit buildings need an in-state interest-bearing account; tenant gets interest less 1% fee | **14 days**, itemized | Missing the deadline forfeits the right to retain anything ([GOL 7-108](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108); [GOL 7-103](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-103)) |
| Massachusetts | 1 month (+ last month's rent) | Separate interest-bearing MA account; bank receipt within 30 days | 30 days, deductions sworn under penalty of perjury | **3× deposit** + interest and fees ([c.186 §15B](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleI/Chapter186/Section15B)) |
| Washington | No cap | Trust account at a WA institution | **30 days** (was 21) with invoices or labor hours | Up to 2× deposit ([RCW 59.18.280](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=59.18.280)) |
| Texas | No cap | Commingling allowed for owners | 30 days after surrender **and** forwarding address | ([Akerman chart](https://www.akerman.com/a/web/65441/Security-Deposit-Laws-Residential-Lease-State-Comparison-Chart.pdf)) |

The broad 50-state source here is a **2020** Practical Law chart, and several rows changed afterward. The rules table needs an owner who re-verifies it, and the UI should say the rules are reference data, not legal advice ([Akerman chart](https://www.akerman.com/a/web/65441/Security-Deposit-Laws-Residential-Lease-State-Comparison-Chart.pdf)). The trigger that starts the return clock differs by state: vacate (CA, NY), surrender plus forwarding address (TX), surrender plus tenant demand (AZ), or termination (MD, DC). Model it as an enum and open a disposition task on move-out day, since the shortest windows are 14 days. The schema hook already exists: the Phase 1 move-out checklist (`lease_checklists.kind = 'move_out'`) can carry the disposition step. The ledger rule is hard: **a deposit liability never closes to income without a disposition record.** That record is an itemized statement, its sent date, and the applications to damage or rent charges.

Two regimes stack, and the module must know which one applies. Landlord-tenant statutes govern owners, and many states let an owner commingle. Real-estate license law governs any broker or manager holding other people's money, and it requires a trust account regardless. California's DRE requires deposit into a trust account within **3 business days** at an in-state bank and treats any shortfall of bank balance against the sum of positive beneficiary balances as a violation. **Negative beneficiary balances may not be netted** ([CA DRE RE 13](https://www.dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/re13.pdf)). Regulators expect a monthly three-way reconciliation: adjusted bank balance = trust book balance = sum of beneficiary ledgers ([ADRE form](https://azre.gov/sites/default/files/Forms/Auditing/Trust_Account_Reconciliation.pdf); [CA DRE RE 13](https://www.dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/re13.pdf)). Open-property's current users are owner-operators, so v1 should treat books as owner-held. It should still type each bank account (operating, deposit trust, reserve) with its bank's state and keep the deposit subledger tied to the deposit-liability account. Agent-held trust mode, with per-owner ledgers, negative-balance disbursement blocks and signed three-way reconciliations, belongs with owner statements later.

Deposit interest needs four calculation strategies:

1. A fixed rate: Minnesota 1%, Ohio 5% on the excess.
2. Actual interest earned less an administrative fee: New York and Pennsylvania 1%.
3. An agency-published annual rate: Connecticut, Chicago, San Francisco, Los Angeles.
4. Landlord election: Florida pays 75% of earnings or 5% simple.

Sources: [Akerman chart](https://www.akerman.com/a/web/65441/Security-Deposit-Laws-Residential-Lease-State-Comparison-Chart.pdf); [Baselane](https://www.baselane.com/resources/state-security-deposit-interest-rate-rules). Interest paid to a tenant reportedly triggers a 1099-INT at **$10 or more**, which means collecting tenant TINs. That threshold comes only from secondary sources ([LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/when-do-you-need-a-w-9-form-from-a-tenant/)). Because the interest machinery only matters in the strict states, defer it until a customer operates there, and block deposit setup with a clear message in those states until then.

## Payables, capex and tax: capture the facts, defer the math

The AP side should be coded at the **bill line**, not the bill. One invoice often mixes repair and replacement, and the IRS de minimis safe harbor applies "per invoice or item": **$2,500** without an applicable financial statement and $5,000 with one, elected annually ([IRS tangible property regulations](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/tangible-property-final-regulations)). Two other safe harbors decide most coding questions. The routine-maintenance safe harbor covers work on the building structure or a building system that is expected more than once in 10 years. The small-taxpayer safe harbor applies at **≤$10M gross receipts and ≤$1M building basis**, with total spend at most the lesser of $10,000 or 2% of basis. Everything else goes through the betterment/adaptation/restoration test, applied separately to the structure and each of **eight building systems** ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/tangible-property-final-regulations)). Tax and lender treatment diverge on turnover items. Pub 527 lists carpet and built-in appliances as improvements ([IRS Pub 527](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527)), while HUD's MAP Guide has carpet budgeted as operating expense where it is not a listed capital component ([HUD MAP Guide](https://gocodebook.com/library/us/federal/us-hud-map-guide-4430-g-2020-rev2021-03-19/appendix-2-quality-control-plans-and-identity-of-interest/a.5.7.4.1-capital-costs-vs.-operating-expense)). So each bill line needs a book classification (opex/capex) plus an optional tax tag (de minimis, routine maintenance, small-taxpayer, capitalize), not one boolean.

Depreciation should be stored, not computed, in v1. Residential buildings and structural components are 27.5-year straight-line with mid-month convention; appliances, carpet and furniture are 5-year; land improvements are 15-year ([IRS Pub 527](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527)). The One Big Beautiful Bill Act restored **100% bonus depreciation for property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025**, so the asset register needs separate acquisition and placed-in-service dates ([IRS Pub 946](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946); [BDO on Notice 2026-11](https://www.bdo.com/insights/tax/irs-issues-interim-guidance-on-bonus-depreciation-rules)). Section 179's "qualified real property" covers roofs, HVAC and similar improvements only on **nonresidential** property ([26 U.S.C. 179](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/179)). The value for apartment owners therefore runs through cost segregation, which is CPA work. A fixed-asset register with class, unit-of-property bucket, dates and source bill lines gives the CPA what they need without the app owning tax computation.

Vendor 1099s changed for 2026 and should be a year-keyed lookup, not a constant. **Payments made in calendar 2026 report on 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC rents at $2,000** (up from $600). Attorney gross proceeds stay at $600, and card or third-party-network payments are excluded because the processor reports them on 1099-K ([IRS Instructions 1099-MISC/NEC](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec)). The 1099-K threshold reverted to over $20,000 and over 200 transactions ([IRS IR-2025-107](https://irs.gov/newsroom/irs-issues-faqs-on-form-1099-k-threshold-under-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-dollar-limit-reverts-to-20000)). Backup withholding is 24% when a payee fails to give a correct TIN ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/backup-withholding)). The `vendors` table needs legal name, TIN type and an encrypted TIN, entity type (corporations are mostly exempt), W-9 date and a default 1099 box. Every payment needs a method, so card payments drop out of 1099 totals. Each income and expense account should carry a `schedule_e_line` so a per-property Schedule E worksheet falls out of the ledger ([IRS Schedule E instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040se)). Retainage, approval routing and owner distributions are real practices, but none is needed by the owner-operators the app serves today.

## Close, controls and lender reporting set the v1 report list

The month-end close has no standard-setter, but practitioners agree on its sequence. Cut off resident activity, post charges, receipts and write-offs, tie the aged-receivables total to the AR control account and prepaids to the prepaid liability, tie deposit activity to the deposit liability on both bases, reconcile every bank account, validate GPR, book accruals, run the package, and **close the period last** ([REdirect Consulting](https://www.redirectconsulting.com/blog/yardi-year-end-close-checklist-2026-step-by-step-month-year-end-process)). Lenders push the close toward accrual. Fannie Mae wants actual expenses normalized for taxes, insurance, reserves and management fees ([Fannie Mae §503.01](https://mfguide.fanniemae.com/node/18131)), and Freddie Mac wants real-estate tax reported as the amount due for the year rather than the amount disbursed ([Freddie Mac PRS CREFC Desk Reference](https://mf.freddiemac.com/docs/prs_crefc_desk_reference_guide.pdf)). In v1 the close can be a period-status machine (open → closed → locked) with a pre-close checklist that runs the invariant checks. Postings dated into a closed period are rejected, and reopening is an audited, privileged act.

Controls are where the app's lack of users bites. Segregation of duties is the core control: the vendor creator should not approve that vendor's invoices, the enterer should not approve, and the bank-rec preparer should not review it ([ManageCasa](https://managecasa.com/articles/accounts-payable-process); [CLA](https://www.claconnect.com/en/resources/blogs/real-estate/protect-what-youve-built-with-internal-controls-for-property-management)). These rules exist because the fraud patterns are concrete. A Connecticut manager hid **at least $275,000** of collected rent by submitting rent rolls that showed occupied units as vacant ([FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/newhaven/press-releases/2014/monroe-man-admits-role-in-rental-income-fraud-scheme)). Until Phase 5 delivers accounts and roles, the enforceable controls are structural: append-only lines, reversal-only correction (`reverses_id UNIQUE`), period locks, no manual postings to control accounts, and an exception report flagging units that have receipts while marked vacant. Build the ledger to record an actor on every entry from day one, even if v1's actor is a placeholder, so Phase 5 does not need a backfill.

Lender reporting fixes the report formats even if the app never transmits them:

| Program | Annual | Interim | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fannie Mae | Form 4254 by **June 1** (12 months actuals) | Q2/Q3 by Sept 15 / Dec 15; none for small loans ≤$6M | Comment required on **≥20% YoY variance** in EGI, opex, capex or DSCR ([Fannie Mae §503](https://mfguide.fanniemae.com/node/18131)) |
| Freddie Mac | A-CREFC-MF by **May 31** (Mar 31 if risk rating >6), borrower-certified | Q2 by Aug 31, Q3 by Nov 30, with a certified T-12 and a rent roll ≤2 months old | Late fees $1,000–$5,000; **$25,000/$50,000** for unacceptable submissions ([Freddie Mac Ch. 40](https://mf.freddiemac.com/docs/chapters/mf_guide_ch_40.pdf)) |

Freddie Mac's rent roll definition lists 12 fields: tenant, unit, contract rent, lease start and end, commercial flag, market rent, subsidy, past-due balance, additional charges, unit type and size ([Freddie Mac Ch. 40](https://mf.freddiemac.com/docs/chapters/mf_guide_ch_40.pdf)). Most already exist in `units` and `leases`. A v1 rent roll and a T-12 operating statement with EGI, NOI and DSCR lines therefore cover the agency-loan owner without building CREFC templates. Retention should default to **seven years** after fiscal year-end for the ledger and subledgers, the IRS bad-debt horizon ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/how-long-should-i-keep-records)), with property and basis records kept until the disposition year closes. That makes hard deletes of posted data a defect, not a feature.

## Affordable housing: support the voucher, not the regulator

Affordable programs split cleanly into accounting the app can do and compliance machinery it should not try to replicate. HUD-insured and assisted properties need restricted cash (operating, security deposit, reserve for replacement, residual receipts). They also need a surplus-cash computation on Form HUD-93486 that gates distributions under each property's regulatory agreement, and GAAP annual statements filed electronically **within 90 days of fiscal year-end** ([24 CFR 5.801](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/24/5.801); [HUD-93486](https://www.hud.gov/sites/dfiles/Housing/documents/93486_orcf.docx)). Monthly Section 8 billing runs through the HUD-52670 voucher, transmitted by TRACS and due by the 10th of the month before payment ([HUD-52670](https://www.hud.gov/sites/documents/52670.pdf); [SC Housing](https://www.schousing.sc.gov/business-and-community-partners/voucher-processing)). HOTMA's multifamily compliance date has moved again, to **January 1, 2027** ([HUD bulletin](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USHUDFHA/bulletins/4008a60)). LIHTC compliance is unit and tenant certification policed through IRS Form 8823, with chargeable rent equal to the limit minus the utility allowance ([IRS 8823 Guide](https://www.treasurer.ca.gov/ctcac/compliance/manual/appendix2/Form8823Guide.pdf)). Yardi and RealPage compete on exactly these certification engines and TRACS connectivity ([Yardi Voyager Affordable](https://www.yardi.com/product/voyager-affordable-housing/)).

For a small-operator product, the high-reach feature is the Housing Choice Voucher landlord, who has no HUD filing obligation. That landlord needs four things: a lease with a third-party payer that splits each rent charge into tenant and HAP portions; bulk receipt of one PHA deposit across many tenant ledgers; abatement entries computed daily (monthly HAP ÷ days in month × days out of compliance) that are not billed to the tenant ([Mass. EOHLC](https://www.mass.gov/doc/hcvabatementmemo/download)); and late-fee logic that never charges a tenant for a late HAP payment. All of it reuses the open-item AR design, which is why the payer belongs on the charge from v1 even if the voucher UI ships later. The HUD chart mapping covers the auditor handoff for the rare HUD property. TRACS, 50059 and tax-credit certification, EIV data (Privacy Act-protected, with its own rules of behavior per [HUD-90012](https://www.hud.gov/sites/documents/90012.pdf)), FASS-MF submission and HFA portals stay out.

## Conclusion

The research reverses the usual intuition about where accounting software is hard. The chart of accounts, the P&L and the lender formats are mostly configuration. The real risk is at the edges where money changes hands: which charge a partial payment pays, when a deposit legally stops being the tenant's money, and whether a deleted payment can silently rewrite last quarter. Each is governed by a state statute or an auditor's expectation, and each fails quietly. The expensive mistakes for open-property are therefore structural and early: shipping Phase 3's `REAL`, cascade-deleted `expenses` table as drafted; letting rent payments stay deletable; or building a posting path the database adapter cannot make atomic.

That implies an order of work that differs from the current roadmap. First, approve the scope change, since `intent.md` excludes a general ledger. Second, rewrite migration 0003 so expenses are integer-cent bill lines that become the AP subledger. Third, replace `payments` with receipts plus applications and migrate history as opening balances. Fourth, add the ledger behind the existing rent, deposit and bill workflows. Running Phase 5's user and role work before or alongside the ledger turns the segregation-of-duties controls from aspiration into enforcement. Jurisdiction rules should be versioned data with a named maintainer, because the deposit and late-fee tables changed in at least four states since the most comprehensive source was published.
